---
name: cross-side-handoff
description: Prefer completion with the current arm; use a sequential tabletop regrasp only when reach, approach or an explicit task requirement requires switching arms.
---

Before picking or switching hands, locate the source grasp region and final placement region from current RGB and privileged_scene. Compare both arms using observation.arm_workspace distances and outer reach bounds, current joint limits, FK directions, required lift height/orientation, clear swept paths and actual tracking. Check the full pickup-to-placement route. Inside an outer reach bound is not a proven feasible pose or path; a center outside it does not exclude a reachable edge. If the current arm can complete the task, keep it and do not hand off. Opposite table sides alone do not justify a handoff. Record the direct-versus-handoff decision and concrete evidence in memory; unresolved reach calls for further assessment of existing geometry or a revised approach.

Switch hands only when a specific reach/approach limitation prevents direct completion or the public task explicitly requires the other hand. First choose a visible tabletop staging area reachable by both arms and record the donor, receiver and reason. All handoffs use placement on the table followed by the other arm's pickup; do not exchange objects directly between hands in midair.

Choose the intermediate area from current RGB in the shared reachable central workspace. It must have flat free support, room for the object, opposing finger access for the receiving arm and room for both wrists to withdraw. The table center is a region to inspect, not a fixed coordinate. Preserve task-required object orientation and keep the destination clear. If the center is crowded, choose another visible overlap area and revise the route before lifting.

The pickup arm carries only to that intermediate support. Confirm retention during motion, lower onto support, open, and withdraw until the object is visibly table-fixed and the receiving approach is clear. Keep the receiving arm clear until release and withdrawal are verified. The receiving arm then re-localizes the supported object, grasps with opposing pad contact, performs a short retention lift and carries it to the final destination. Use transport-retention throughout. This is a sequential supported handoff; never release an unsupported object between hands.

For orientation-sensitive objects, retain the original task reading direction and any observed staging rotation in memory across release and regrasp. A stable receiving grasp establishes retention, not correct orientation: compare the object’s distinctive features with its original orientation or the task reading frame. If release dragged or rotated it, keep that correction pending until an observed adjustment or clear comparison resolves it. Inspect with the fingers clear when they occlude the shape; correct while safely held above support or regrasp from supported rest before final placement.

If already carrying and the destination approach repeatedly stalls or makes negligible progress, reassess reachable height, orientation, approach geometry and both arm assignments. Continue with the current arm if a clear feasible route remains. If the receiving arm resolves a specific limitation, retain the grip while returning to the selected tabletop support, stage, release and withdraw before its pickup. If no safe shared tabletop area is available, revise the plan rather than attempting an airborne exchange. Reserve simulation time for staging, regrasp, final placement and required home return.

When a tabletop handoff is needed, a successful intermediate release completes only its first half. Continue to the public task's actual final condition after the receiving grasp; for a placement task this includes final support and finger withdrawal, followed by reset when required. Tasks that require a held lift instead keep the object retained at the required height; do not add a placement or home return they do not request.
