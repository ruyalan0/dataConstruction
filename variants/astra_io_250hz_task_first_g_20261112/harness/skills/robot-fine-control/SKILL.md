---
name: robot-fine-control
description: Use RGB and robot proprioception to execute short contact-sensitive movements, diagnose tracking residuals, and recover from failed manipulation without hidden state.
---

At every segment compare current and previous RGB, requested and achieved wrist pose, object motion and gripper contact. A commanded pose is not an achieved pose. A closed gripper is not proof of holding an object.

Choose transit only with visible clearance along the entire swept gripper and wrist attachment. Within contact range, descend, rotate, close, lift and release as separate fine segments. Review RGB after each segment before continuing. A rotation sweeps the attachment and can translate an object held away from the wrist. If the wrist stalls or unexpectedly drifts, stop advancing into the obstacle; identify a visible escape direction, retreat a small distance and reassess. Repeating a blocked target with more steps is not an established remedy.

For X5, end-effector pose reports wrist link6. Finger contact is approximately 0.138m along wrist local +X. For a desired contact c and orientation R, wrist position is c - R*[0.138,0,0]. Top-down R=Ry(90deg): local +X points down. Rz(yaw)*Ry(pitch) sets grasp/approach orientation. Quaternions are wxyz. Preserve the desired gripper command during transport; observed finger opening is not a replacement command.

Use the arm on the object's side when reachable; left and right have different reachable orientations. Empty left wrist commonly uses yaw180, right yaw0 at pitch90. These are starting hypotheses, not permission for a large unseen rotation. Maintain original home poses across controller restarts.

Evidence: frozen v11 layouts10/12 failed fixed-right reach; bilateral-v5 rotation produced unobserved wrist drift; bilateral-v4/v7 lowering stalled; bilateral-v6 slower lower worked once but did not solve the whole task. Short model-observed correction is a new candidate, not yet a proven improvement.

Recovery revision v2 (candidate, evidence dev-skill-001 steps315..434): two successive commands in the same direction that achieve little progress while pose residual persists are a blocked approach, even when each residual is below a hard stop threshold. Compare achieved displacement, not only absolute error. Change approach orientation or arm after retreating to clear space; repeated shorter descents at the same pose are not a new strategy. Use the observed quaternion when estimating contact center: target orientation may no longer match the wrist during a stall.

Keep desired gripper commands as explicit episode memory. A nonzero observed opening with command0 often indicates an object physically resisting the fingers. Preserve command0 during transport; omit the gripper field to hold the last command. Change it only for intentional release or regrasp. In dev-skill-001 steps230/236 the model copied observed~0.376 into the desired command, despite earlier closure0; this was an error, not a calibrated grasp setting.

Observation context revision v3: desired_gripper_commands is the last executed command, reconstructed from the robot's action history after restart. contact_estimates use the achieved wrist quaternion, not the requested one. Compare requested_displacement and achieved_displacement to distinguish progress from repeated stall. These are derived proprioception, not object truth. IK rejection, self-collision, object collision and insufficient settling can all cause a residual; name the uncertainty rather than declaring the lid is the cause.

Budgeted observation: use fine segments during contact, within a few centimeters of contact, or while recovering a stall. Once an empty gripper has visibly cleared objects and its retreat tracks, select transit for the next clear waypoint instead of dozens of fine moves. Keep every contact segment observed. Dev-skill-001/recovery-01 spent190steps without opening the lid; overusing fine in clear space is an observed cost, not additional demonstrated safety.

The executor checks achieved robot state after every native step. It interrupts a segment on more than35mm tracking residual, or more than0.06 opening change from the segment start when a previously closed gripper had nonzero opening. It then returns RGB to Astra. Interpret this as a request to inspect, not proof of slip or collision. Account for the partial achieved path and choose the next command from actual pose. wrist_error_m compares the achieved wrist with the last executed interpolation target; unexecuted_target_distance_m is the remaining command, not tracking failure. After interruption, reassess retention and clearance before deciding whether any of that remaining path is still appropriate. Dev004 at360 interrupted3 of18 steps; comparing against the final waypoint falsely reported22cm error. Earlier interruptions288/289 returned RGB but did not rescue that grasp.

Choose observation spacing from the next contact, not from a ritual list of phases. In clearly empty space, move to a waypoint a few centimeters before the next visible obstacle in one transit segment; near contact keep fine observations. Finish closure and its inspection separately from the test lift. A short successful retention test can be followed by a clear-space transfer with the guard active, while still checking object clearance.
