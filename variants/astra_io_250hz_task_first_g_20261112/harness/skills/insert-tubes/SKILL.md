---
name: insert-tubes
description: Grasp and orient three tubes, align their lower ends with separate rack openings, and verify seating and release before withdrawal.
---

Maintain a visual map of the three tubes and rack openings. Identify each tube's capped end and narrow insertion end from head and wrist views before choosing a grasp and rotation. Grasp the body so enough of the lower end remains exposed to enter the rack while the fingers clear its rim. Confirm retention with a lift before reorienting in open space.

Before closing on an unheld tube, align the open fingers across its body using the current tube pose, not a remembered tabletop orientation. Distinguish the wrist's local +X finger-extension/approach axis from its local Y finger-closing axis. A cross-body grasp places the tube approximately along wrist local Z, perpendicular to both axes. Prepare orientation in clear space before approaching; never open a suspended held tube merely to adjust alignment.

After each new pickup and independent short test lift, check grasp skew before rack transport or upright reorientation. From this observation's normalized wxyz quaternions, let t = R_tube * [0, 1, 0], a = R_wrist * [1, 0, 0], and c = R_wrist * [0, 1, 0], all unit vectors in the same world frame. The tube's local Y is its longitudinal axis. Compute theta = degrees(acos(clamp(abs(dot(a, t)), 0, 1))) and approach deviation = 90 - theta; also compute closing deviation = degrees(asin(clamp(abs(dot(c, t)), 0, 1))). Absolute dot products make either tube-axis sign equivalent for this check; cap-up versus tip-up must still be resolved separately. Use measured wrist orientation, never the requested joint goal. If root orientation is unavailable, use the longest current mesh principal axis only when tube identity and geometry are unambiguous; a world-aligned bbox or a single projected image angle is not a substitute for a 3D tube axis.

Use 10 degrees as the initial maximum for each deviation. This is a development heuristic, not an evaluator threshold or proof of a secure grasp. If either deviation exceeds 10 degrees after lifting, preserve the grip and confirm with one further distinct, settled observation before committing to a regrasp; an unchanged control_arms hold can supply that observation. Do not count two views of the same step as two measurements. Missing, near-boundary or contradictory evidence requires inspection, not an automatic release. Require lift clearance, observed retention and no slip as well as acceptable angles; moves_with_gripper alone does not prove secure contact. Recheck after a new grasp or relative slip, not merely after rigidly rotating a retained tube. Rotating the wrist and tube together cannot correct their relative grasp angle.

For confirmed excess skew, use a supported regrasp rather than continuing the rejected grasp into the rack. Choose a visible stable tabletop patch away from rack openings and seated tubes, with clearance for support, release, withdrawal and the next approach. Lower under retained grip until support and settling are observed, then open, withdraw, and re-localize the tube. Change the pregrasp orientation and, when useful, body grasp height so the fingers close across the body while leaving the insertion end exposed. Close in a separate action, repeat the independent test lift, and recompute both deviations. Never drop a tube, release it unsupported, or attempt a direct airborne handoff. A different arm, when specifically justified, must acquire it from confirmed table support under the existing handoff rules.

Allow at most one supported regrasp per tube for this angle gate; keep the tube identity, measured deviations, confirmation steps, attempt count and actual geometry change in memory. Estimate support/release, retreat, reacquisition, test lift, insertion and final home-return time from the remaining simulation budget before starting recovery. Do not repeat the same rejected geometry or exhaust the episode on angle correction. If the regrasp still fails the gate, do not mark it accepted or force insertion: leave the tube safely supported and pursue another task-permitted tube if feasible. When time is insufficient, prioritize supported placement and feasible withdrawal/reset without disturbing completed tubes; budget pressure never justifies an unsupported release. Apply this gate to newly acquired tubes, not already released rack-supported tubes.

Choose an unoccupied opening and a grasp that leave clearance for the whole gripper through seating. Previously seated caps stand above the rack rim: check the fingers and wrist attachment against those caps, not only the held tip against the opening. If the hand would meet a cap before the lower end is guided, lift clear and choose a changed grasp or opening from current views, using supported regrasp when needed. Align the tube's lower end with the opening's interior while the tube is upright. A gripper center over the opening is insufficient when the tube sits off-center between the pads. Resolve tip and rim occlusion with another current view or an empty-hand observation move before contact. Preserve previously seated tubes and a clear withdrawal direction.

Descend axially in short observed segments. Compare the cap against both the rack and the finger pads: motion relative to the pads can indicate axial slip, while a stationary tube and rising tracking error indicate blocked insertion. For a rim catch, lift enough to free the tip, correct lateral alignment and try again; pushing harder at the same offset can tilt the rack or tube.

Use cap height and rack overlap as evidence of seating, accounting for differences in tube length and grasp height. A wrist height copied from another tube does not establish insertion depth. When the upright tube is guided by the opening and supported, open the fingers and observe settling. Confirm the tube stays upright in the rack as the empty hand withdraws without sweeping across neighboring caps.

Repeat for all three tubes. Record accepted placements and their last unobstructed views. Finish with open grippers and a clear return to the observed home configuration, then check native completion.
