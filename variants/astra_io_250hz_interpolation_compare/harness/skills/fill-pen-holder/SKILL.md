---
name: fill-pen-holder
description: Hold the pen holder with one hand, insert four pens tip-up with the other, then return the upright filled holder to the table and reset.
---

Assign one hand to the holder and one to the pens. Side-grasp the holder below its rim so the mouth stays visible and accessible. Keep it upright and in a reachable loading position with clearance for the inserting hand. Preserve the established holding grasp while the other hand picks and loads one pen at a time.

Identify each pen's writing tip and opposite end before rotating it: the task requires tip-up insertion, with the opposite end entering the holder first. Choose the grasp for the final insertion pose: the entering end must project beyond the finger pads and palm far enough to reach support inside the holder while the hand stays clear of its rim. A secure lengthwise grasp can leave that end buried beside the palm. Inspect this relationship before transport; if the hand would reach the rim first, return the pen to visible support and regrasp. Confirm a test lift, then rotate the pen in clear space away from the holder and other arm. Choose the grasp and rotation direction together so repeated large inversions are unnecessary.

Align the entering end over the opening, allowing for the shaft's offset from the finger center. Lower the upright pen through the free part of the opening. As pens accumulate, choose space between the existing shafts instead of driving the new pen against them. For a broad or clipped marker, fit its whole cross-section and clip through that space; an entering-end point inside the mouth does not clear the rest of the body. Choose its yaw from the visible gap before insertion. On rim contact or a tilted shaft, lift clear and correct alignment before continuing downward.

Release when the lower end is safely inside and supported, then withdraw without dragging across the rim or neighboring pens. Confirm the pen stays in the holder rather than following the hand. A pen can remain at the mouth after release while still perched on the rim or other pens. Inspect its lean and exposed length relative to that pen's observed body, and resolve a suspected shallow or wedged insertion before returning the filled holder. Track four distinct pen identities, tip direction and observed release evidence; a shaft hidden by the rim is not by itself proof of retention or full insertion.

After all four are visibly retained tip-up, lower the upright holder to a clear tabletop support, open its grasp and verify stability during withdrawal. Preserve the pens' clearance during this last transfer. If a pen starts rotating or sliding outward during a holder adjustment, stop the holder motion and reassess that pen's support before continuing the return; keep it pending until reseated or recovered. Return both hands open to their observed home configuration and check native completion; loading four pens without the final return is incomplete.
