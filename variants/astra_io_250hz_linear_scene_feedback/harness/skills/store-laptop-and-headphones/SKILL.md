name: store-laptop-and-headphones
description: Hang the headphones, close and extract the laptop from its support, then orient and seat it in the vertical rack before returning home.
---

Preserve the public sequence: hang headphones, close the laptop, lift it from its stand, insert it into the vertical rack, then return home. Treat the two stands as distinct objects. Complete and release the headphone placement before approaching the laptop.

Choose an opposed grasp that leaves the central inner headband available for the stand's cradle and allows the fingers and wrist attachment to withdraw. The public demonstration illustrates a grasp at the side near an ear cup; choose the actual contact surfaces from current RGB and confirm retention with a test lift. Inspect the curved band, ear cups and whole gripper when planning the approach. If the retained grasp repeatedly puts a finger or wrist attachment into the cradle's path, use a clear supported staging pose to change the grasp instead of repeating the same blocked approach.

Orient the actual headphones in clear space with the ear cups below the upper arch. Plan how the whole cradle will enter the headband opening. For an overhead descent, first clear the stand with both cups and the wrist attachment, then compare the cradle footprint with the passage between the cups. A centered arch does not establish that this passage is wide enough. If the cups block that entry, retreat clear and choose a visibly open yaw, oblique or lateral entry through the arch, or use supported staging to change the grasp. Small offsets that still bring a cup onto the cradle repeat the same failed entry; obtain evidence of a changed clear passage before another descent.

Once the cradle is inside the opening beneath the inner upper band, lower in observed segments while keeping the cups and whole gripper clear. Contact against the side of the cradle does not establish that the band can remain hanging after release. Confirm support beneath the inner band and room for the cups to hang. Open without dragging the band, withdraw the entire empty gripper through a clear route, and observe that the headphones remain independently supported after settling. If they fall, update the scene state before continuing the required task sequence.

Close the laptop by contacting the lid away from the hinge while its base remains supported. Follow the lid's closing arc in short segments, checking that the base stays on the original stand. A gripper pressing on the lid is not yet a carrying grasp.

Before extraction, locate an exposed laptop edge and the stand rails underneath it. Plan a pad on each side of the closed laptop thickness with a visible route for the lower pad beneath the edge. Reduce the open gap to fit the available clearance while keeping both pads outside the object until aligned. An empty closure or a single finger compressed against the stand does not capture the laptop. After a blocked entry, withdraw and change edge, approach or arm; repeating descent into the support consumes the insertion budget.

Choose an accessible overhanging edge. If neither hand can reach an opposed grasp, first assess whether a short supported shift can expose an edge while keeping the laptop's support footprint on the stand; inspect after each such contact. Use a second grasp or a supported handoff when needed for stable rotation. Lift free of the original stand before turning the closed laptop upright.

Align the laptop's lower edge along the vertical rack slot, center its thickness between the guides, and lower in short axial segments. On an edge catch, lift, realign and retry without forcing the rack. Verify the rack supports the laptop after release, preserve the hanging headphones, and return both empty open hands to home.
