---
name: push-t
description: Push the T with low horizontal contact while preventing tipping and preserving the observed target orientation.
---

Read the visible target pose and the T's directed stem/crossbar orientation. Record both in memory before contact. Select an accessible side face and an in-plane push direction whose swept footprint is clear.

Arrange the compact empty gripper at the desired low contact height while it is laterally CLEAR of the T. Approach the side horizontally. During contact, change horizontal wrist position while keeping its measured height and attitude approximately constant through suitable joint changes. Do not descend onto the top face or use a vertical tap to acquire the side: top contact can lever an edge upward. The contact point must remain on a side face above the table, not underneath an overhang.

Separate coarse translation from final orientation correction. While the target is far away, select a broad accessible side whose push line passes near the observed center of the T footprint; a stem tip or crossbar corner supplies a large rotational lever. After one short pulse, compare both center displacement and directed-axis change in a view of the whole T. Continue that contact only while it produces the intended translation. If it mainly spins the block, change the contact point before another pulse. Once near the target, use the short tangential pushes for angular correction. Keep the empty observer wrist positioned to see the complete stem and crossbar when the pushing arm blocks the head view.

Unload along the reverse of the last contact approach until a visible tabletop gap separates the entire finger sweep from the T. Then change sides through clear space. A tangential reposition made before true separation can catch the stem and undo an achieved angle even with perfect height tracking. Recheck the block after withdrawal, not only at the end of the push.

Use short pushes and inspect all visible lower edges. If an edge lifts, the T tilts, or the fingers slide onto its top, stop and retreat horizontally to unload; reposition in clear space before reapproaching. For an orientation change, move to another side and use short tangential in-plane pushes. Do not try to twist the T using downward pressure. Recheck the directed T orientation as well as its center against the target, withdraw horizontally, then lift away only once clear and return home.

Evidence: task-specific development revision from the operator's 2026-09-21 review and prior failure observations. This is a strategy candidate, not a demonstrated success; resolve concrete poses from the current episode only.
