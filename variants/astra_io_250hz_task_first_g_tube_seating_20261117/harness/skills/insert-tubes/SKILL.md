---
name: insert-tubes
description: Grasp and orient three tubes, align their lower ends with separate rack openings, and verify seating and release before withdrawal.
---

Maintain a visual map of the three tubes and rack openings. Identify each tube's capped end and narrow insertion end from head and wrist views before choosing a grasp and rotation. Grasp the body so enough of the lower end remains exposed to enter the rack while the fingers clear its rim. Confirm retention with a lift before reorienting in open space.

Before closing on an unheld tube, align the open fingers across its body using the current tube pose, not a remembered tabletop orientation. Distinguish the wrist's local +X finger-extension/approach axis from its local Y finger-closing axis. A cross-body grasp places the tube approximately along wrist local Z, perpendicular to both axes. Prepare orientation in clear space before approaching; never open a suspended held tube merely to adjust alignment.

After each new pickup and independent short test lift, check grasp skew before rack transport or upright reorientation. From this observation's normalized wxyz quaternions, let t = R_tube * [0, 1, 0], a = R_wrist * [1, 0, 0], and c = R_wrist * [0, 1, 0], all unit vectors in the same world frame. The tube's local Y is its longitudinal axis. Compute theta = degrees(acos(clamp(abs(dot(a, t)), 0, 1))) and approach deviation = 90 - theta; also compute closing deviation = degrees(asin(clamp(abs(dot(c, t)), 0, 1))). Absolute dot products make either tube-axis sign equivalent for this check; cap-up versus tip-up must still be resolved separately. Use measured wrist orientation, never the requested joint goal. If root orientation is unavailable, use the longest current mesh principal axis only when tube identity and geometry are unambiguous; a world-aligned bbox or a single projected image angle is not a substitute for a 3D tube axis.

Use 10 degrees as the initial maximum for each deviation. This is a development heuristic, not an evaluator threshold or proof of a secure grasp. If either deviation exceeds 10 degrees after lifting, preserve the grip and confirm with one further distinct, settled observation before committing to a regrasp; an unchanged control_arms hold can supply that observation. Do not count two views of the same step as two measurements. Missing, near-boundary or contradictory evidence requires inspection, not an automatic release. Require lift clearance, observed retention and no slip as well as acceptable angles; moves_with_gripper alone does not prove secure contact. Recheck after a new grasp or relative slip, not merely after rigidly rotating a retained tube. Rotating the wrist and tube together cannot correct their relative grasp angle.

For confirmed excess skew after a new tabletop pickup, use a supported regrasp rather than continuing the rejected grasp into the rack. Choose a visible stable tabletop patch away from rack openings and seated tubes, with clearance for support, release, withdrawal and the next approach. Lower under retained grip until support and settling are observed, then open, withdraw, and re-localize the tube. Change the pregrasp orientation and, when useful, body grasp height so the fingers close across the body while leaving the insertion end exposed. Close in a separate action, repeat the independent test lift, and recompute both deviations. Never drop a tube, release it unsupported, or attempt a direct airborne handoff. A different arm, when specifically justified, must acquire it from confirmed table support under the existing handoff rules. For contact-induced skew during insertion, use the confirmed rack-supported local regrasp below when accessible; tabletop support remains the fallback when that local route is unavailable.

Allow at most one supported regrasp per tube for this angle gate; keep the tube identity, measured deviations, confirmation steps, attempt count and actual geometry change in memory. Estimate support/release, retreat, reacquisition, test lift, insertion and final home-return time from the remaining simulation budget before starting recovery. Do not repeat the same rejected geometry or exhaust the episode on angle correction. If the regrasp still fails the gate, do not mark it accepted or force insertion: leave the tube safely supported and pursue another task-permitted tube if feasible. When time is insufficient, prioritize supported placement and feasible withdrawal/reset without disturbing completed tubes; budget pressure never justifies an unsupported release. Apply this gate to newly acquired tubes, not already released rack-supported tubes.

Before the first insertion, choose three unoccupied openings with sufficient spacing for the whole gripper and seated caps. Order them along the rack's observed left-to-right direction, not by opening ID or a fixed world axis. Choose either left-to-right or right-to-left from current reach and clearance, and record the direction and ordered openings in memory. Insert at the starting end first, the middle selected opening second, and the far end last. Never fill both ends before the middle. Tube pickup order and arm assignment must not change this opening order.

After each verified seating, release and withdrawal, advance only to the next opening in the chosen direction. An occupied opening with unresolved insertion depth is still the current opening: finish its seating or its bounded local recovery before acquiring another tube. Local correction of this current placement preserves the chosen opening order. Keep the gripper's approach and withdrawal on the unoccupied side of the growing group rather than threading between seated caps. If the next opening is obstructed, lift clear and inspect; substitute only a safe opening farther along the same direction and update the remaining plan. Do not reverse direction, backfill between seated tubes, or force a blocked insertion. If safe progression is unavailable, keep the held tube securely retained or place it on confirmed support, and reassess without disturbing completed tubes.

Treat each placement as align, descend, verify depth, release, verify settled seating, then withdraw and advance. Keep the current tube pending until its depth, uprightness and release stability are verified. Rack guidance or a stationary tube alone does not establish seating. Keep the working hand near the current opening with cap clearance until this check passes, instead of returning home with a known shallow insertion.

Detect shallow insertion from current tube angle and height separately. Let t = R_tube * [0, 1, 0] be the cap-up longitudinal direction and n the rack's current upward normal; compute upright tilt alpha = degrees(acos(clamp(dot(t, n), -1, 1))). This is distinct from the tube-to-wrist grasp-skew gate. Locate the actual narrow insertion end from the current tube geometry and views; do not substitute the wrist center or an unverified object origin. Measure its depth along n below the chosen opening plane, and compare its distance to the rack's lower support with the expected seated geometry. Check the tip's lateral alignment with this specific opening as well. For tubes with the same geometry, compare cap and tip heights to a verified seated tube after accounting for tilt; never copy another tube's wrist height or assume different tube lengths match. A depth deficit greater than about 0.0100 m is an initial shallow-insertion heuristic even when the tube looks upright. Tilt above about 15 degrees, increasing tilt during descent, or a cap that stops descending warrants inspection; tilt alone is not proof of inadequate depth and a stable, sufficiently deep placement need not be disturbed for a small lean. These are development heuristics, not evaluator thresholds. If depth geometry is uncertain, inspect it without advancing the placement ledger.

During descent, compare actual tube progress, tilt and lateral alignment between distinct observations. A stalled descent with rising tilt or pad-relative slip triggers local correction before release; do not repeat a blocked downward goal at the same offset. Preserve a retained grasp, lift along the opening enough to disengage the caught end, then correct lateral alignment and the tube's world tilt in clear space before a short observed reinsertion. Recheck retention after contact-induced slip. Rigid wrist rotation can correct world tilt only while the retained tube follows and grasp skew remains acceptable; it cannot repair an excessive tube-to-wrist grasp angle. Confirmed excessive grasp skew still requires a changed supported regrasp before reinsertion.

After release, allow one short settling observation with the fingers clear. If height remains shallow or tilt is still increasing, act immediately on this current placement rather than repeatedly waiting, acquiring the next tube or going home. For a rack-supported tube, use the clear unoccupied side to align open fingers across the exposed body, close without a downward shove and verify retention. Lift just enough to free the tip before turning it upright and realigning over the same opening; then descend in short axial segments, release and verify depth and stability again. The rack may provide support for this changed regrasp when that support is confirmed; do not automatically take a rack-guided tube through a full table recovery. If a retained grasp fails the angle gate, establish rack support before release, change the across-body acquisition geometry and repeat the independent short lift and angle checks. Preserve neighboring caps and the original opening direction; never reach through a filled gap to repair a placement deferred from earlier in the sequence.

Allow at most one local seating recovery per tube, record its trigger, measured depth/tilt, changed geometry and result, and count a supported regrasp against the existing per-tube regrasp limit. Reserve a local-correction interval and final withdrawal/home time before starting the last insertion; use observed action durations to estimate both, rather than budgeting a complete tabletop pickup for every local correction. If bounded recovery fails or its route is obstructed, leave the tube on confirmed support or retain it securely and explicitly keep it incomplete. Once a shallow pose is stationary, repeated unchanged holds will not seat it. Repeated identical observations must lead to the chosen feasible recovery or an explicit budget-limited fallback, not consume the remaining time while claiming completion.

Choose an unoccupied opening and a grasp that leave clearance for the whole gripper through seating. Previously seated caps stand above the rack rim: check the fingers and wrist attachment against those caps, not only the held tip against the opening. If the hand would meet a cap before the lower end is guided, lift clear and choose a changed grasp or opening from current views, using supported regrasp when needed. Align the tube's lower end with the opening's interior while the tube is upright. A gripper center over the opening is insufficient when the tube sits off-center between the pads. Resolve tip and rim occlusion with another current view or an empty-hand observation move before contact. Preserve previously seated tubes and a clear withdrawal direction.

Descend axially in short observed segments. Compare the cap against both the rack and the finger pads: motion relative to the pads can indicate axial slip, while a stationary tube and rising tracking error indicate blocked insertion. For a rim catch, lift enough to free the tip, correct lateral alignment and try again; pushing harder at the same offset can tilt the rack or tube.

Use cap height and rack overlap as evidence of seating, accounting for differences in tube length and grasp height. A wrist height copied from another tube does not establish insertion depth. When the upright tube is guided by the opening and supported, open the fingers and observe settling. Confirm the tube stays upright in the rack as the empty hand withdraws without sweeping across neighboring caps.

Repeat for all three tubes. Record accepted placements and their last unobstructed views. Finish with open grippers and a clear return to the observed home configuration, then check native completion.
