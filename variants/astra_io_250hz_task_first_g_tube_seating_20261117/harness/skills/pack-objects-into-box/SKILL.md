Read the current instruction and identify each object's front from visible shape or appearance. Keep a checklist of objects, front-direction evidence and confirmed placements. Preserve the meaning of left from the scene's initial frame as wrist cameras move; a wrist image's left edge is not a new task direction.

Inspect the box edges, available interior space and raised flaps. Flaps can extend above and outside the rim: plan clearance for the whole gripper and wrist attachment during approaches, rotations and empty-hand withdrawals. Establish the required box alignment before crowding the interior, and keep that alignment during placement.

Unexpected box movement during a supposedly clear approach or withdrawal requires a contact and clearance check even when joint tracking is accurate. Withdraw through visible clearance and relocalize the box. Before picking the next item, check that its insertion and the hand's withdrawal remain reachable at the current box position. If displacement has removed that route, use a free hand for a controlled table-supported box adjustment, preserving task alignment and checking already packed contents afterward. Stage a held object on visible support first when carrying it obstructs this correction.

Choose a grasp that leaves the front identifiable and allows rotation in clear space. After a retention lift, orient the actual object's front leftward using head and wrist views; wrist orientation alone does not establish the object's orientation within the fingers.

Choose an interior position with room for the entire object, its release and finger withdrawal. Plan long or bulky items so they do not block later insertions. Carry with clearance above the rim, then lower in observed segments. If the box moves, the object catches the rim or retention changes, stop pressing, inspect and correct from clear space. Relocalize the box after any movement instead of using its old position.

Release onto support and withdraw along a clear path. Confirm the object stayed fully inside and kept its left-facing front after settling. A drop can change orientation, and an object balanced over the rim is unfinished. Inspect the box and previously packed objects after any corrective contact. Use a supported regrasp if orientation needs repair; avoid repeated rotations of a held object against crowded walls.

After all placements are confirmed and the box remains aligned, clear both empty hands, open the grippers and return both arms to their supplied initial poses. Reserve native steps for this final inspection and reset, then check native completion.
