---
name: put-bottles-into-dustbin
description: Prepare an open side grasp behind standing bottles without contact, advance horizontally around the body, then close and test lift; retain verified body-aligned grasps for lying bottles and supported disposal handovers.
---

Read the full instruction and locate all four bottles, their standing or lying orientations, the table edge and dustbin opening from current RGB. Assign visual identities and track each as on-table, held, staged or deposited. Determine which arm has a clear approach above the bin; the shifted table makes cross-body carrying unsuitable for some sources. Plan pickup and bin-side placement roles before each grasp.

For a bottle on the opposite side from the bin, use cross-side-handoff: source-side pickup, supported release in a visible shared table region, withdrawal, then bin-side regrasp and disposal. Choose support away from the table edge with room for the full bottle and both finger approaches. If a lying bottle can roll, inspect its settled position after release before receiving. A central bottle may be picked directly by the bin-side arm.

For a STANDING bottle, use four observed phases: SET SIDE POSTURE -> STAGE BEHIND -> ADVANCE -> CLOSE AND TEST LIFT. Complete each phase and inspect its achieved result before proceeding. Keep the visibly empty gripper open until the bottle body is between the pads. Apply this sequence again when regrasping a bottle staged upright for handover.

SET SIDE POSTURE: Choose a graspable neck or body section that fits the jaw opening and a clear horizontal approach corridor. While still safely separated from the bottle, open and orient the gripper for a side grasp: its measured approach axis points horizontally toward the bottle, parallel to the tabletop, and its pad-to-pad closing direction crosses the upright body horizontally. Complete wrist reorientation in clear space before moving to the pregrasp point. Check the full arm, both fingers, wrist and camera attachments throughout the planned rotation and transit; clear endpoints alone do not establish a clear sweep. Select the posture from this bottle's current geometry and measured robot state rather than copying joint angles from another scene.

STAGE BEHIND: In the achieved side-grasp posture, move to a pregrasp point directly behind the bottle along the chosen approach line, at the selected grasp height. Here "behind" is the gripper's starting side of the forward approach toward the bottle, not a fixed world axis or the far side of a camera image. Align the open grasp channel with the bottle body, but stop with both fingertip ends still short of its near surface and a visible gap. Keep the bottle and its neighbors untouched during positioning; detour through clear space when a straight transit would sweep across them. Use fresh head and wrist RGB, measured wrist orientation, FK direction references and contact_estimates to check lateral alignment, height and whole-gripper clearance. These are approximate geometric references, not contact measurements. Stop at this separated pregrasp point for an observation before advancing; do not combine staging, final approach and closure in one action.

ADVANCE: From the checked pregrasp point, preserve the side-grasp attitude, grasp height and lateral alignment while advancing horizontally toward the bottle in short observed joint-goal waypoints. Let the two open pads pass on opposite sides of the upright body until the selected section is centered within their closing region. Keep fingers, wrist and attachments clear of the table, cap, shoulder and neighboring bottles. The forward motion positions the open pads around the body; it must not push the bottle with a fingertip, palm or wrist. Do not add a vertical descent or wrist rotation beside the bottle. Check the achieved path after every waypoint: joint-goal interpolation does not guarantee a straight Cartesian approach. If alignment drifts, clearance is uncertain, or the bottle moves before closure, stop, back out through the approach corridor and re-localize before trying a revised pregrasp. Count repeated preparation without progress as a failed approach and change the geometry or arm rather than cycling through the same waypoints.

CLOSE AND TEST LIFT: Once fresh views and measured geometry show the pads on opposite sides of the selected body section, stop forward motion and close in a separate control_arms call while holding the achieved arm pose. Inspect closure, then use another call for a short vertical test lift. Verify that the bottle rises and stays fixed relative to the pads before carrying; an empty-hand lift is a failed acquisition. This standing-bottle sequence uses a horizontal side approach rather than an overhead alignment, fixed-midpoint wrist rotation or vertical-down descent.

For a LYING bottle, re-localize its settled long body axis, then set and VERIFY the empty gripper's orientation above the bottle before descending. The measured approach axis must be perpendicular to the tabletop and point down. In the table plane, align the grasp channel between the two fingers parallel to the bottle's long axis, so the pad-to-pad CLOSING direction crosses the body perpendicular to that axis. Parallel channel alignment does not mean closing along the bottle's length or pinching its two ends. Choose opposing side contacts on a suitable body region, centered between the pads, with finger tips and wrist attachments clear of the table. Complete yaw and tilt adjustment in clear space, check achieved attitude with robot state and current views, then descend vertically in short waypoints without rolling or sweeping sideways against the bottle. If it rolls, tips or shifts, invalidate the old alignment, withdraw clear and re-localize before another attempt.

Use achieved wrist orientation, joint/FK direction references and contact_estimates to check these approaches; a wrist position above the bottle alone does not place the fingers correctly. Close only after the pose-specific alignment and opposing-pad clearance have been observed. Use visual-contact-control to close separately from the test lift. Verify the bottle moves with the fingers before transport; a closed command and lifted empty wrist are not retention evidence. If a standing bottle is accidentally knocked over, stop the current approach and switch to the lying-bottle alignment checks rather than chase it with the previous standing grasp.

Use transport-retention while carrying and account for the complete bottle length below or beside the fingers. Preserve the intended gripper command. If a high carry stalls and lower space is visibly clear around the entire bottle, test a short lower route before continuing. If the distant bin approach still stalls, return toward reachable table support and hand over rather than repeating cross-body targets. Preserve support before any empty-hand posture adjustment.

Carry above the bin with the full bottle footprint inside the visible opening and clear of the rim. A controlled release into the opening satisfies disposal without an unobserved ballistic throw. Lower only as far as needed for a clear drop while keeping fingers outside collision with the rim, then open and withdraw. Inspect that the bottle enters and remains inside; a rim catch, bounce outside or bottle still in the fingers remains unresolved. Recover a reachable misplaced bottle with a revised approach before counting it deposited.

After each confirmed deposit, update the four-object ledger and verify with both grippers open. Keep completed contents undisturbed and use fresh RGB to find the remaining bottles. Independent empty-hand preparation may run alongside a clear transfer only when the swept spaces stay separate; reserve the shared handoff and bin approach volumes for one arm at a time.

When all four bottles are visibly deposited, open both grippers, withdraw through clearance and return both arms to their recorded original poses. Check native completion; if it remains absent after settling, follow the common observation-grounding procedure. Reserve simulation time for the final withdrawal and home return; intermediate handoff or release alone does not prove disposal.
