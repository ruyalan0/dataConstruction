Read both number cards from current RGB and associate each with its red button using the visible layout. Resolve an ambiguous digit with another view before touching any button. Record the two target counts, card/button image associations, both original arm poses, current task stage and observed completed press counts in episode memory. Treat the two red buttons as separate counters.

Follow the explicit public sequence: press the first red button its card's count, press and release blue once, press the second red button its card's count, press and release blue again, then return home. The official scoring row specifies these two confirmations, despite the abbreviated description saying blue ends the task immediately. Reserve blue for those two phase boundaries and use fresh observation before advancing either boundary.

Use visual-contact-control to target the visible button cap with a compact fingertip contact. Choose an arm whose approach and withdrawal leave the cards and neighboring buttons clear. Reach to a pre-contact point in clear-space transit, then press along the cap's travel direction in a short fine segment. Inspect cap movement against its fixed housing. Withdraw enough to let the spring return fully, and observe the raised cap before beginning another press. Holding a button down is one press; command completion alone proves neither depression nor release.

Before the first contact, select an oblique view that exposes both the cap sidewall and a fixed housing feature throughout its travel. A nearly overhead cap top with only a thin dark crescent behind it does not establish a reliable travel reference: changing camera pitch can change that crescent without pressing. Close the empty hand in free space, then choose fingertip offset and wrist orientation that leave the chosen sidewall exposed. Prefer a stationary head or other wrist view when the pressing wrist would rotate or obscure this evidence. In clear space, make one small noncontact approach and check that the same housing feature can still be identified; change the view before contact if the supposed gap cannot be separated from perspective.

Treat cap height and the 0.138 m fingertip offset as uncertain geometric references. Near contact, choose small joint goals intended to move the fingertip only a few millimetres along the cap's travel direction, and inspect achieved movement before adding depth. The controller decides execution time; small requested joint changes still require visual clearance checks. Preserve the raised, candidate depressed and returned observations in memory with camera and decision-point references.

Track each cycle as ready, depressed or released. Increment that button's completed count only once when an observed depression is followed by an observed return. Compare the same housing feature across the three observations, accounting for camera pose. A raised cap after retreat establishes release but cannot prove that the earlier ambiguous contact depressed it. Preserve the phase and evidence across controller continuation so an interrupted down/up cycle is resolved before another press. If the critical contact frame is no longer supplied, retain its unresolved status in memory; remembered pixels from a rotating wrist cannot reconstruct a missing contact observation. If contact is occluded, change the view or withdraw for inspection; avoid a blind extra press to compensate for uncertainty. If two short descents do not depress the cap, retreat and change contact geometry or arm instead of pushing the housing.

At each target count, withdraw clear of that red button and inspect the memory ledger before moving to blue. Execute one observed blue depression and complete release, then advance the phase. Between presses, keep enough vertical clearance that lateral travel cannot touch another button. Use separate arm motion when independent reaches interfere or one arm's blocked approach prevents useful progress.

After the second blue cycle, clear all caps, open the grippers and return both arms to their original poses. Use normal verification until native termination. The target is the exact instructed count and confirmation sequence; an uncertain or overshot count remains a failure to diagnose, not a reason to assert completion.
