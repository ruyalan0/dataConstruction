---
name: push-t
description: Align the T's center and directed orientation with short horizontal pushes, recheck after local withdrawal, and budget final arm return without abandoning feasible corrections early.
---

Read the visible target pose and the T's directed stem/crossbar orientation. Record both in memory before contact. Select an accessible side face and an in-plane push direction whose swept footprint is clear.

At each push and post-withdrawal check, use the supplied current T and target poses to calculate signed xy displacement, planar distance and quaternion angular distance; q and -q represent the same orientation. Geometric alignment requires BOTH planar distance strictly below 0.0070 m and angular distance strictly below 7 degrees. Keep a margin for rounded inputs and contact drift. Cross-check the directed stem/crossbar and corresponding edges in an existing view that shows the whole block and target; a cropped or occluded wrist image cannot certify overall alignment. Use the wrist views for local contact, separation and lower-edge support. Record the remaining translation direction, signed planar angular correction, height/support evidence and how both errors changed after the previous push. These geometric checks do not establish official success or excuse lifting the block.

Arrange the compact empty gripper at the desired low contact height while it is laterally CLEAR of the T. Approach the side horizontally. During contact, change horizontal wrist position while keeping its measured height and attitude approximately constant through suitable joint changes. Do not descend onto the top face or use a vertical tap to acquire the side: top contact can lever an edge upward. The contact point must remain on a side face above the table, not underneath an overhang.

Separate coarse translation from final orientation correction. While the target is far away, select a broad accessible side whose push line passes near the observed center of the T footprint; a stem tip or crossbar corner supplies a large rotational lever. After one short pulse, compare both center displacement and directed-axis change in the existing views of the whole T. Continue that contact only while it produces the intended translation. If it mainly spins the block, change the contact point before another pulse. Once near the target, use short tangential pushes for angular correction. Move an empty wrist camera only if a specific blocked question about stem/crossbar pose cannot be answered from the supplied views and geometry. Once answered, reassign both arms; the other arm may become the pushing arm when its contact approach is better.

Unload along the reverse of the last contact approach until a visible tabletop gap separates the entire finger sweep from the T. This is a LOCAL withdrawal for inspection and possible recontact; keep the empty hand near the working area with clearance. Then change sides through clear space if needed. A tangential reposition made before true separation can catch the stem and undo an achieved angle even with perfect height tracking. Recheck the block after withdrawal, not only at the end of the push.

If either alignment check fails, choose a concrete next contact side, push direction and short waypoint before deciding to finish. For residual translation with acceptable orientation, prefer a push line near the footprint center that preserves the directed angle. For residual angle with acceptable translation, choose a short tangential push with a suitable lever arm and monitor center drift. Recheck BOTH errors after every pulse; if the intended error does not improve or the other grows, unload and change contact geometry rather than repeat the same failed push. A stable, flat block or one corrected dimension alone does not make final support complete.

Before the next correction, compare budget.remaining_simulation_seconds with the estimated time for recontact and a short push, safe withdrawal, BOTH arms' required home route, and a tracking/settling margin. Estimate from measured joints, necessary clearance waypoints, current controller bounds and observed motion durations; update after every return. For linear interpolation, the largest joint delta divided by controller.joint_velocity_limit_rad_s is only a reference-time lower bound; include partial-segment settling and physical tracking. Do not reuse a fixed four-second reserve from an older, slower controller. State the proposed correction and time estimates in assessment or memory; time remaining alone does not prove that a contact route is feasible. Keep all durations as planning estimates, not control_arms arguments.

Begin FINAL withdrawal and home return after both geometric alignment checks pass and post-contact stability is observed. If the remaining budget cannot accommodate a feasible correction plus withdrawal and return, or no clear correction route can be established after comparing contact geometry, return safely and explicitly retain the unmet condition in memory. Required reset, an unknown official result, or a failed push alone is not a reason to abandon a feasible budgeted correction. Do not return home merely to discover whether a known out-of-tolerance residual might be accepted.

Use short pushes and inspect all visible lower edges. If an edge lifts, the T tilts, or the fingers slide onto its top, stop and retreat horizontally to unload; reposition in clear space before reapproaching. Do not try to twist the T using downward pressure. For the final return, withdraw horizontally, lift the empty fingers only once separated, then follow clear waypoints to observation.home_joints.left and .right. Inspect measured arrival and native completion. If completion is absent, identify the unmet condition and reassess a correction-and-return route against the remaining budget; repeated home commands or stationary waits cannot remove geometric residuals.

Evidence: task-specific development revision from the operator's 2026-09-21 review and prior failure observations. This is a strategy candidate, not a demonstrated success; resolve concrete poses from the current episode only.
