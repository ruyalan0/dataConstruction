Read the runtime category-to-basket assignment first. Keep a compact episode inventory with each visible object, category, assigned left/middle/right basket, and whether placement has been visually confirmed. Basket ordering is from the initial head view; preserve that assignment while wrists move. Multiple objects may belong to one category: completing a category requires all of them.

Choose the arm with a clear approach and reach to both object and destination. Estimate grasp height and geometry from head and wrist views; use the shared contact-control skill for wrist/contact offsets. For elongated objects choose a narrow body section with opposing finger clearance. Approach above the object, descend with short observed corrections, close, then make a small test lift. Confirm the object rises with the fingers before a long transport. If the hand rises alone, reopen and revise the grasp from RGB.

For an outer basket, prefer its same-side arm when that arm can also approach the object. When object access and destination reach favor different arms, consider a supported transfer: place the object on a clear, stable tabletop patch visible and approachable by both hands, open, verify it stays on the support during withdrawal, then regrasp with the destination-side arm. Infer the patch and approach from current RGB, leaving finger clearance and avoiding other objects. This costs an extra grasp, so compare it with remaining simulation time and current reach evidence. If a held cross-body transfer repeatedly rejects or stalls despite a changed height/approach, return toward reachable support while retention is still visible rather than spending many wrist rotations at the reach limit. A supported transfer is unfinished until the object reaches its assigned basket.

Keep failed grasp attempts in episode memory with the object, arm, approach and observed outcome. After two empty closures or blocked descents on the same object, withdraw to visible clearance and select the other arm or another unfinished object. A small tilt followed by the same blocked descent still belongs to that failed approach. Revisit only after identifying a concrete change in finger clearance, object pose or reach from fresh RGB. Recompute the remaining object and reset plan after each failed attempt; completing one accessible object can change the scene enough to make a later attempt useful.

Thin wrapped bars need the finger pads to oppose across the short dimension near the body center. A package caught diagonally or near an end can survive a short lift and still rotate out during transfer. Compare its angle and position within the fingers after each initial lift; progressive rotation plus narrowing measured fingers is slipping. Recenter and choose a different closing direction on the next attempt, using the newly observed pose after it lands. Table-blocked descent is not a reason to force the wrist lower.

Transport above the basket rim with clearance for the entire held object. Aim inside the basket footprint, away from the rim and already placed objects. Lower until the object can settle inside without the fingers colliding with the rim; open and verify the object moves independently or stays behind as the fingers withdraw. Choose the withdrawal direction from visible clearance and achieved tracking. If an empty-hand upward segment stalls, test a short retreat toward clear space outside the basket before lifting again, preserving the achieved orientation. Check that the released object remains fully inside. An object balanced across a rim or still held by a finger is unfinished. Correct misplaced objects before declaring that category complete.

Plan across the remaining simulation-time budget. Use useful joint-goal waypoints in clear space and small intermediate goals near grasping, rim passage, and release. Reserve time to inspect all baskets, open both grippers, and return both arms to their observed initial poses.
