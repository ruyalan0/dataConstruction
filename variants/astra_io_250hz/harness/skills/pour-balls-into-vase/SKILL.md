name: pour-balls-into-vase
description: Transfer seven balls from a cup into a vase by aligning the pouring lip, observing the fall path, and returning the emptied cup upright before reset.
---

The task requires all seven balls inside the vase, the cup upright and the robot back at its observed origin. Identify the vase mouth, the cup opening and a grasp region that leaves its pouring lip unobstructed. After a retention lift, move the upright cup along a path that clears the vase with the whole cup and wrist attachment.

Bring the pouring lip over the vase mouth before sustained tilt. Account for the lip moving as the cup rotates; the wrist can remain still while the opening moves away from the vase. Use short tilt increments near the rim and inspect the visible ball path before extending a pour. Keep enough clearance for the cup body throughout its rotation while avoiding an unnecessarily high drop.

Establish a view of the receiving mouth and the space just outside it before balls begin exiting. If the carrying wrist or cup hides that region, use the empty opposite hand for an oblique view from clear space or adjust the approach while the balls are retained. Seeing balls leave the cup does not show where they land. Near first exit, use brief observed intervals; if landing becomes unreadable or balls pass outside the mouth, reduce tilt toward the last observed retaining orientation through a clear sweep, then resolve the alignment before sustaining another pour.

When balls are visibly falling into the mouth, hold the stable pouring pose for observed intervals. Inspect the cup for balls caught behind its inner lip and the tabletop for escaped balls. A visibly empty cup supports transfer, but does not establish that every ball entered the vase. If a remaining ball is visible inside the cup, adjust tilt in small clear increments with the lip still over the mouth.

For recovery of an escaped ball, verify retention before transport and ground release over the current mouth from more than one viewing direction. A projection onto an assumed rim-height plane leaves height-dependent lateral uncertainty. Resolve that uncertainty before releasing above a narrow opening, and minimize the drop while preserving visible clearance for the ball and fingers. Inspect the actual fall and settled location before counting the recovery as complete.

Return the cup upright while its swept volume is clear of the vase. Identify the recessed mouth and closed base before lowering; when their identity or the cup's tilt is unclear, compare its original upright appearance or obtain a clear oblique view. Seeing the empty interior alone does not establish uprightness. Carry it upright to a visible supported tabletop location, release, and verify that it stays behind and remains upright on its base as the fingers withdraw. A cup can be independently supported on its side or rim; resolve visible leaning or rolling before the arm returns home. Return the empty open hand through the clear approach corridor to its observed home. Use the native completion signal rather than a visually estimated count when ball visibility is incomplete.
