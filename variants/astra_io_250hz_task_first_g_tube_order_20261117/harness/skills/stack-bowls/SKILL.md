name: stack-bowls
description: Acquire stable radial rim grasps, retain bowls through transport, and build an upright nested stack at a reachable site.
---

Inspect each bowl's rim, wall profile, table support and reachable approach. Choose a stack site both arms can reach without extreme extension. The nearest stable bowl can become the base; relocate it on clear table support if the originally selected destination requires an awkward reach. Record identities and current placements separately from intended roles.

For a rim pinch, align the finger closing direction approximately radially across the chosen local rim, with one pad inside and the other outside. Use a slightly tilted gripper pose, with its approach axis a little off the table normal, instead of a fully vertical top-down grasp. Choose the tilt from the visible rim and wall geometry so both pads can seat across the wall. Set the tilt and radial alignment with the EMPTY gripper in clear space before descending; a tangential closing direction can fail to oppose the thin wall. Use a narrow enough pregrasp that the inner finger does not strike the bowl's bottom or opposite wall. Approach the rim in small observed vertical segments. If the open hand tips or shifts the bowl, withdraw and relocalize its rim before trying a shallower, better-centered approach. Do not continue descending from an uncertain height estimate.

Close at a stationary pose and perform two short near-vertical test lifts. A nonzero measured finger gap supports contact but does not establish retention. Require the rim to remain fixed relative to the pads while the table moves in the image. If the bowl pivots steeply, slips along a fingertip or its rim leaves the pad gap, return to visible support and choose a deeper pad contact, a different rim segment or a different arm. If useful, the empty hand can gently support an exposed outer edge while the holding hand levels the bowl; keep fingers clear of the eventual nesting gap.

Inspect all existing views and state to resolve pad depth; move an empty wrist camera only if the unresolved pad-contact question blocks execution and a new view can answer it. Two-dimensional overlap with the finger gap does not establish that the wall lies between opposing pad faces. A closure to the empty-hand gap with the bowl stationary is a miss; small lateral changes followed by the same miss are still the same failed acquisition strategy. After two acquisitions without progress, compare a different rim/contact geometry, a different unfinished bowl and switching grasping arms. Choose a concrete change; do not keep one arm permanently observing repeated attempts. Once the needed evidence is obtained, reassign both arms. If the nearest bowl remains difficult, leave it as the supported base and acquire a more accessible bowl. A steadily narrowing measured gap and a rim creeping toward the pad tips during test lifts indicate sliding; reseat on support and improve pad engagement before a larger lift.

Only control_arms is available; do not attempt temporal triangulation or projection tools. Use the existing paired views and supplied geometry first. If a specific unresolved contact question blocks execution, temporarily move an empty wrist to the needed side view, inspect the result and then reassign both arms.

Before lateral transport, inspect clearance of the bowl's LOWEST edge over every neighboring rim. A high wrist is insufficient when a rim-held bowl hangs tilted. Maintain the confirmed grasp orientation through the transfer; do not combine a marginal grasp with a large yaw/roll change near another bowl. If an unheld bowl moves, stop the lateral sweep, gain visible clearance and update its position. Never continue toward an old destination after contact has moved it.

Bring the carried bowl level and approximately concentric over an upright, stationary base, lower in short observed segments, then release gradually onto nesting support. Inspect the stack after the hand withdraws; three overlapping silhouettes alone do not establish upright support. Preserve completed nesting while placing the final bowl, open both hands, and return both arms to their observed home poses.

Evidence: historical L20 lifted a bowl by step 55 but lost it after transfer/contact by step 83 and suffered later repeated slips. L21 changed an initially tangential approach to a radial rim grasp, retained it through two lifts, and chose a more reachable base. These observations motivate the strategy; layout and provider differed, so they do not isolate one causal factor. No historical pose or action sequence is supplied.
