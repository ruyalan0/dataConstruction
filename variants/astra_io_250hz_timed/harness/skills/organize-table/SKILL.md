Make a visual checklist of each object and its destination: mouse on pad, keyboard inside its frame, figurine on stand, clock on top of drawer, then the remaining miscellaneous objects inside the opened drawer. Track supported release separately from grasp and transport. Follow the full current instruction and keep every unfinished item on the checklist.

Inspect the keyboard's long edges and the frame opening before pushing. First align the keyboard with the frame while it is supported on the table, then advance along the opening with short observed contacts. Pushing a diagonal keyboard farther into a corner can wedge it. When one corner stops and the other rotates, withdraw, correct alignment with a contact on the exposed edge, and inspect again. Keep the mouse and other completed placements outside the hand's sweep.

For the clock and figurine, identify an opposed grasp on the object body with finger clearance from its feet, stand or surrounding support. A shallow pinch at the top can close without retaining the body. After an empty lift, reopen and relocalize the body sides from current views; change the contact depth, closing direction or arm before another attempt. Transport only after visible retention, then lower to destination support and verify that the object stays there as open fingers withdraw.

Open the drawer by its visible handle along its observed sliding direction, keeping the clock stable above it. Inspect the exposed interior before inserting miscellaneous items. Distinguish the drawer front from the handle and avoid pulling the whole cabinet. If motion stalls, inspect contact and clearance before increasing travel.

After each completed placement or failed grasp, compare remaining steps with unfinished items and the final return home. Use transit segments in observed clear space and fine segments at contact. After two attempts with the same failure, work on another reachable unfinished item or use a visibly different approach. Preserve verified placements, inspect the full checklist with empty hands, then return both arms to their supplied initial poses and check native completion.
