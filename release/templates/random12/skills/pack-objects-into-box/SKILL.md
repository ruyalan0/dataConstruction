Read the public instruction. Identify each object and its front from visible shape or appearance. Keep a checklist of front evidence, placement and remaining uncertainty. Preserve scene-left from the initial view as wrist cameras move. Success requires every object fully inside with the requested facing, the box aligned, and both empty open hands returned to their supplied initial poses.

## Plan from the destination back to the grasp

Inspect the box floor, rigid walls, raised flaps and the wrist mounting bracket. A fingertip path above the low rim can still drive the wider attachment into a side flap. Distinguish the lower entrance between flaps from the tall flap edges. Select the carrying hand, wrist attitude and grasp together so the whole assembly can reach release height and leave again, with the bulky attachment facing open space. Include the held object's ends in this plan. When clearance is unresolved, inspect from a useful wrist view or check the empty-hand approach before loading it.

Keep an already aligned, reachable box stationary. If adjustment is necessary, do it while the interior is empty and hands are free: use visible rigid-wall contact and a short table-supported adjustment, then release and check the actual box pose. A raised flap is an obstacle, not evidence of a usable handle or movable hinge. Check that the selected insertion route remains reachable before picking an object.

Plan the interior slots before loading: leave space for bulky objects and for the fingers to open beside each placement. Favor a roomy, reachable slot for the most clearance-sensitive object while the box is empty. Inspect each placement before another object obscures it.

## Keep facing and hand clearance compatible

Choose opposed contacts that retain the object while leaving its front identifiable and enough of it below the fingers to reach support. Test retention above the table. Orient the actual front left in clear space and verify from head/wrist RGB; wrist yaw alone does not prove object facing.

If the left-facing object forces the bracket into a flap at release height, change the grasp or carrying hand. Set the object on clear tabletop support in the desired orientation, release, and regrasp from a side that permits the planned insertion. This separates object facing from hand attitude. Recheck retention and facing after the new grasp before approaching the box.

## Separate transit, insertion and withdrawal

Complete lateral travel and substantial wrist rotations in clear space, with the entire swept assembly above or outside the tall flaps. Then approach the selected opening with the planned hand attitude. Lower in short observed segments, keeping both object ends inside the footprint and the bracket clear. A raised wrist does not establish clearance for a lower protruding finger or attachment.

Unexpected box movement is evidence to revise the route even with accurate joint tracking. Stop the advance and disengage through the observed entry corridor; inspect which part of the assembly was near the flap or wall. Before retrying, change that clearance relation through approach side, hand attitude or supported regrasp. Merely relocalizing the moved box and repeating the same low sweep leaves the interference unresolved. Stage a held object before any necessary box correction.

Release only after the object is supported fully inside. Open without lateral motion, inspect separation, then withdraw along the clear entry corridor until the fingers AND mounting bracket have cleared the flaps. Only then turn toward the next object. Confirm that the released object stayed inside, retained its left-facing front and did not disturb earlier placements. A drop, rim-balanced object or occluded facing remains unresolved.

Reserve steps for final inspection and empty-hand return. After the checklist is satisfied, clear both hands, open them, return both arms to their initial poses and check native completion.
