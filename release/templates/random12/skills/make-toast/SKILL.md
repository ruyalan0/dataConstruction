name: make-toast
description: Deep-grasp fresh bread, present its broad face near horizontal for an opposite-side handoff, verify receiver retention before turning for slot insertion, and replace dropped slices.
---

Inspect the slices in the source shelf/basket, toaster slots and clear approach directions. Choose a fresh supported slice and target its broad central body, not a shallow pinch on the crust or a corner. Center the opposing pad contact over the slice's middle, keeping the lower insertion edge clear. At the position that would ordinarily look ready to close, treat it as a PRE-GRASP: keep the fingers open and advance another approximately 30–40 mm along the actual entry direction in short observed segments. Confirm that the pads reach farther onto the central face on opposite sides before closing. This extra entry is relative to the visually estimated pre-grasp, not a fixed world-coordinate downward move. Keep the shelf, toaster and table clear; contact, slice tipping or stalled motion calls for unloading and realigning the central approach, not forcing the remaining distance. Close only after central overlap is visible, then make a short test lift and verify that the slice moves with the pads relative to the background.

If a slice drops flat onto the tabletop, mark it dropped and leave it there. Do not spend repeated attempts pinching or scooping it up, and do not use it for the next insertion. Withdraw from the fallen slice and select a NEW slice still supported in the source shelf/basket. Keep fallen slices in the obstacle map, but do not count them as completed or as still on the shelf. Do not deliberately lay down a held slice as an intermediate handoff surface. If no fresh slice remains accessible, report the task incomplete rather than silently switching back to tabletop recovery. The public completion condition also requires exactly two slices left on the shelf; taking a replacement after a drop may make that condition unattainable. With an accessible fresh slice and sufficient remaining action budget, continue the operator-requested replacement even if full completion is no longer attainable. Report the actual remaining inventory and unfinished conditions; that inventory mismatch alone is not a reason to stop before replacing the dropped slice.

**Use a broad-face presentation for an airborne handoff.** Arrange this in visible free space clear of the shelf and toaster. With the receiver still separated, turn the retained slice so its broad face is approximately horizontal or gently inclined, with an exposed opposite side accessible to the receiver. Determine this from the slice itself, not wrist angles alone. Plan two separated broad-face contact regions: the donor occupies one side, the receiver takes the exposed interior of the other half. Each grip should cover bread body rather than just a crust corner, while leaving room between the two hands. Keep both palm housings outside opposite sides of the slice.

Hold the donor closed and stationary. Align the receiver's open gap with the slice thickness: one finger above and one below the broad faces, with finger extension and approach roughly along the slice plane. Approach from the exposed edge opposite the donor, then advance in short segments onto the free broad-face region. Check the lower finger has cleared beneath the slice and the upper finger passes above it, rather than a fingertip pushing into the broad face or sweeping the donor pads. The 30–40 mm extra-entry rule above is for the supported source pickup; during handoff, use the available exposed overlap and hand clearance to choose depth, rather than blindly adding that distance toward the donor. If the slice deflects, pivots, or the donor's measured opening changes unexpectedly during receiver approach, unload the receiver along its clear approach. Reorient the retained slice or receiver while they are separated before trying again.

Close the receiver without arm motion and inspect the opposed broad-face contact. The lower contact may become occluded after entry; use the observed entry geometry and the controlled transfer below rather than repeatedly repositioning just to expose the hidden pad. Keep the receiver still while gradually easing the donor; check that the slice stays registered with the receiver as donor support is removed. Only then open the donor enough to clear the bread and withdraw it a short distance along its entry corridor. Establish receiver retention with a short lift after the donor fingers are clear. Turn the slice upright and align for the toaster only after this separation; rotating while both hands still engage the slice can lever it out. If receiver support is not established, retain donor support and correct the approach. A dropped slice triggers the fresh-slice rule above.

Before entering the toaster, orient the slice plane along the slot's long axis with its thin dimension across the narrow opening. Check both ends of the lower edge from head and an unobstructed wrist view. Make angular corrections above the slot, then lower in short observed segments. A tilted slice can jam with one corner inside while much of the bread remains high. If descent stalls, the slice leans, or one end rests on the rim, lift free before correcting pitch/yaw/roll; additional downward pressure is not alignment. Release only after the slice is visibly captured evenly by the slot and no longer bridges the rim. Follow the public lever/toasting sequence, inspect both slices, and return both arms home.
