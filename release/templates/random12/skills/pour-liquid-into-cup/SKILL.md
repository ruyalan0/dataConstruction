---
name: pour-liquid-into-cup
description: Pour one bottle into one cup using a middle-body grasp, an observing empty arm and inward tilt; drain under observation and restore the bottle upright.
---

Identify the bottle and cup from the current instruction and RGB. The public goal is enough liquid in the cup and an upright bottle. Draining the bottle is the strategy, not proof that the cup received enough. Reserve native steps for grasping, observed pouring and upright recovery; return the arms home only if the current instruction requires it.

1. **Choose the arm for the whole pour.** Use the robot's workspace, not image left/right: prefer the left arm for a cup on the left and the right arm for a cup on the right. For a central cup, choose the clearer bottle-base, wrist and forearm sweep through tilt and upright return. Let that arm pick up the bottle directly when the approach is clear. If pickup or transport requires the other arm, use one supported handoff: place the bottle upright in clear shared reach, release and withdraw, then re-localize and grasp with the pouring arm. Verify the receiving lift before treating the handoff as complete.

2. **Observe pickup and grasp the broad middle body.** Position the EMPTY arm at a clear oblique view showing the bottle, approaching fingers and cup rim. Keep it stationary outside the pickup corridor. Identify base, broad body, shoulder, narrow neck and mouth. Open enough to clear the broad body and put opposing pads roughly halfway between base and shoulder, below the narrow neck. Check actual pad placement and clearance before closing; a neck grasp is not the intended body grasp. If the available opening cannot clear the body, reassess the grasp's feasibility rather than forcing the fingers through it. Confirm retention with a short upright lift, then a small rotation in clear space: the bottle must follow the pads, not pivot inside them. Return an unstable bottle to visible support and correct the grasp before transport.

3. **Set up a low outlet and an inward sweep.** Stage the held bottle outside the cup: left arm from the cup's left, tilting the mouth rightward into it; right arm from its right, tilting leftward. Judge the actual bottle motion, not a memorized joint sign. A small preparatory tilt can raise the base before a tall bottle approaches a low cup; keep the base, fingers and wrist clear of the rim, handle and table, and bring the mouth over the interior before flow begins.

   Establish the EMPTY arm's pouring view before that tilt. It should expose the mouth, near and far inner rim, landing area and bottle/gripper clearance. Keep the observer outside the full tilt-and-return sweep and liquid path; the pickup view may need one repositioning. Use it together with the head view to resolve depth, with localization tools when needed. Aim for a low visible mouth-to-rim gap and a flow path into the interior with margin. A wrist center above the cup, or a stream overlapping its opening in one image, does not establish this alignment.

4. **Tilt a little, hold, then inspect actual flow.** Keep the observer still while making short inward rotations. Compensate translation for the mouth's arc around the body grasp. Advance short native hold steps at the achieved pose so flow can develop; API waiting does not advance drainage. When fresh liquid visibly enters the cup, hold that pose and observe. If it hits the rim, an arm or the table, reduce tilt to stop the spill, wait over the cup for residual drops, then correct the mouth position before resuming. Extra tilt is not a correction for a missed landing. Cup motion or bottle slip calls for unloading the contact and revising the approach. If observation becomes blocked, pause further rotation and restore the empty arm's view through clear space.

5. **Follow stopped flow with further draining.** When visible flow stops at an intermediate angle, keep the mouth over the cup, add a small inward tilt, hold and inspect again. If flow resumes, let it drain before the next increment. Continue toward near-inverted vertical: base above mouth, mouth pointing downward, judged from the bottle itself. At that posture, successive clear views of no outgoing liquid separated by a short native hold support an indirect empty assessment. Visible residual liquid still requires draining; an occluded mouth or uncertain inclination remains unresolved. If the draining posture cannot be reached through a clear sweep, recover upright and record unresolved drainage rather than repeating blocked rotations. Record whether the empty assessment is direct, indirect or unknown, separately from evidence that liquid entered the cup. Liquid already in the cup or a single no-flow frame is not sufficient evidence of emptying.

6. **Restore the bottle upright and verify the outcome.** Keep the observer clear and the mouth over the cup while smoothly reducing tilt to catch residual drops. Watch the base's downward sweep and bottle-to-pad retention. Confirm the actual body and neck are upright in world space, not merely vertical in a rolled wrist image, then check native completion. If stable uprightness needs support, set the base on a clear tabletop patch, release and verify the bottle stands as the fingers withdraw. Preserve the cup throughout. Report the native outcome and any unresolved filling or drainage evidence; repeated emptying motions cannot recover liquid already spilled.
