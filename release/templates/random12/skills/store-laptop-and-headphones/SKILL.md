Hang and release the headphones before manipulating the laptop. Then close the laptop, extract it from its support, place it in the separate vertical rack, release and return both empty open hands home. Resolve object positions, contact surfaces and paths from current RGB and public robot state. The user identifies these headphones as a rigid loaded object: prefer controlling the whole headset through one ear cup rather than expecting the cups or band to flex around the cradle. This prior does not make the laptop hinge rigid; inspect actual lid motion.

### Grasp and hang the headphones

Prefer an opposed grasp across the solid body of one ear cup, with enough of both pads on its opposing surfaces to retain the whole headset. Distinguish the broad cup from its thin connecting stalk and headband. Leave the central inner arch exposed and choose a wrist attitude that will keep the entire hand outside the cradle's insertion and withdrawal path. Approach an accessible cup while the headset is supported, close at a fixed wrist pose and test a short lift. Confirm the cup and distant band move together relative to the table and stay fixed relative to the pads. Before large reorientation, test a short rotation as well: stable finger opening alone does not establish rotational retention. If the headset pivots or slips, return it to visible support and revise pad overlap or the chosen cup.

Orient the retained headset with the arch above the cups in clear space. Inspect the cradle's full shape and a continuous entry path into the opening beneath the inner arch. A rigid headset cannot widen its cup gap by squeezing or pushing the cradle between obstructing cups. For overhead entry, both cups and the entire gripper must pass around the cradle. If that passage is too narrow, approach the opening laterally or obliquely from a visibly clear side, so the cradle enters under the arch without a cup crossing its solid top. Plan entry and later empty-hand withdrawal together. After a cup or wrist catches, lift or reverse the observed approach to free it, then change the path or supported grasp enough to reveal a clear passage before trying again.

Once the cradle is inside the opening, lower until it visibly supports the inner upper band, with the cups hanging clear. Ease the grip at fixed wrist pose, observe a real pad gap and stationary supported headset, then open fully. Withdraw the whole hand along the planned clear route and check the headset remains independently hanging. A cup touching the cradle, a vertical silhouette or a stable pose while still gripped is not this completion condition. Restore a fallen headset before proceeding to the laptop.

### Close while supported and establish a carrying grasp

Use the supported-closing route for this edition. Keep the base on its current stable tabletop or original stand while closing; moving an open laptop to a lower surface is unnecessary when its existing support is secure. Before touching the lid, identify the hinge, exposed base edges, underlying rails and a reachable grasp after closure. If pressing would slide the base, use the other hand to stabilize an accessible base edge while leaving the hinge and lid sweep clear.

Contact the outer lid away from the hinge and follow its closing arc toward the keyboard in short segments. Watch actual lid angle and base support; translation of the whole laptop calls for unloading and stabilizing its base. Continue until the lid meets the base along the visible edges. Withdraw the pressing hand enough to distinguish closed support from an edge grasp.

For pickup, approach an exposed overhanging edge from the side with pads opposing the top and underside of the closed thickness. First see a clear route for the lower finger below the edge and above the support. Advance until both pads overlap the laptop surfaces sufficiently to carry it, rather than pinching only the rounded rim. Check that the lower pad grips the laptop itself, not a stand rail. When rails obstruct entry, withdraw and change accessible edge or arm; a short supported shift may expose an edge only while the laptop remains securely supported. A laptop lying flush on the table may have less lower-finger clearance than on its stand, so select the support and approach from visible geometry.

Close at fixed pose and make a short translation test above the original support. Confirm the laptop follows while the stand stays and the grasped edge stays in the same place relative to both pads. Before a large rotation, use the free hand to support or grasp a separate exposed edge if the first contact is shallow or asymmetric. The receiving grasp should capture the closed lid and base together and leave the eventual rack-entering edge free. Keep the original hand until the second contact supports the laptop through a short easing test, then withdraw it clear.

### Rotate and insert with retained support

Lift clear of the original stand before turning toward the rack. Rotate in short prefixes and compare the actual laptop edges with the pads after each one. Edge drift, pivoting inside the grip or one finger closing while the other opens means rotational retention is uncertain even when the laptop initially rises with the hand. Hold the achieved attitude and establish the second hand's opposed support, or reseat on visible support to deepen the grasp, before further turning. Continuing the rotation after observed slip does not establish a better grasp.

Align the actual lower edge with the vertical rack's slot, with the closed thickness centered between its guides and the fingers outside the insertion path. Resolve the rack separately from the original laptop support. Lower along the slot in short segments. On an edge catch, lift along the observed entry and correct alignment; inspect lid closure and retention if the laptop moves relative to the pads. Confirm the rack carries the laptop before easing and opening. Withdraw without dragging it, verify the headphones still hang and the laptop remains closed and independently seated, then return both empty open hands to their observed initial home poses and verify native completion.
