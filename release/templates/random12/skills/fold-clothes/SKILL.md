From the initial RGB, identify the collar, shoulder edge, sleeve ends and two hem corners. Preserve image references and appearance cues for these landmarks. Cloth deforms: later wrist locations and a compact silhouette cannot establish where those material points ended up.

Fold each sleeve inward toward the opposite chest area. Pinch the visible sleeve end, make a small retention lift, move it across the supported garment and release. Inspect the material end after the fingers clear. Avoid dragging the torso or pulling the other sleeve back open; stabilize supported fabric with the other hand when current views justify it.

Before folding the lower panel, reacquire both actual hem corners and the current shoulder line. At each flat hem corner, inspect where the outer hem seam crosses the open jaw gap. Position that edge between the opposing inner finger surfaces; seeing fabric below the tips in a top view does not establish this overlap. If the overlap is obscured, use an oblique permitted view and a short empty-hand approach adjustment. A small additional descent can correct a visibly high pinch; stop descending if measured motion suggests table contact.

After closing, make a small mostly vertical test lift and check that both identified corner seams lift away from the tabletop with the hands. A gathered interior patch at the tips while the outer hem stays on the table does not establish a retained corner. Keep each end pending until its material edge follows the lift; support the cloth and regrasp a missed end before forward folding. With two retained hem grips, fold the lower panel toward the shoulders, keeping the hem edge approximately parallel to the shoulder edge and avoiding uneven tension. Reaching an estimated shoulder coordinate with the wrist is not evidence that the hem corner reached it.

Lower the folded panel onto support, release and withdraw enough to see the result. Compare the released hem ends and edge direction with the shoulder ends and line, and check that both sleeve folds remain inward. Trace the hem boundary from a recognizable seam or pattern patch recorded before lifting; the nearest visible wrinkle or compact outer contour may be a different material edge. When overlapping layers still hide an endpoint after the hands clear, obtain an oblique permitted view before treating the fold check as complete. Carry the unresolved endpoint identity forward explicitly if inspection cannot establish it. Correct a visibly trailing hem corner or skewed edge with a supported small adjustment rather than pulling the whole folded bundle. Reserve time for this inspection before the final return home.

Once the material folds are supported and visually checked, open both hands and return to their supplied initial poses through clear space. Use native completion as the verdict; a small bounding box or accurate joint tracking alone does not prove a correct fold.
