The opponent's discarded tile and your three identical hand tiles form the kong. Complete two manipulation stages: tip those three hand tiles flat with their patterned faces exposed, preserving all other standing tiles; then draw the replacement tile from the separate side stacks, reveal its face and stand it at the free right end of your remaining row. The replacement is a separate draw, not the opponent's discard.

Apply the task procedure and manipulation precautions across all make_kong layouts. Choose tiles, arm roles, contact points, the airborne handoff location and placement targets from current RGB and public robot state. Trigger corrections from observed contact, retention and alignment; layout IDs or seeds do not select a strategy or supply scene answers.

### Identify and expose the matching hand tiles

Wait with empty hands clear until the opponent's discard has settled and its arm has withdrawn. Read the discarded face and identify all three exact matches in your row by pattern and markings. Record their visual identities and positions relative to neighboring tiles. All non-matching row tiles must remain upright; identify them from the current scene.

Tip one identified matching tile at a time into the clear area in front of the row. Use head and active-wrist views to resolve its side edges, adjacent non-matching tiles and falling path. Choose a narrow contact near the upper edge, within the target's width. Check that the other finger, palm, wrist housing and arm also clear neighboring tiles. Approach from clear space above, lower to the selected contact, then make a short push in the intended falling direction so the tile pivots about its lower edge. Avoid a broad sideways sweep across the row.

Inspect the target and its neighbors after each short push. If a neighboring tile starts to move or lean, stop, withdraw clear and reassess contact before continuing. Lift away before moving laterally to the next target. A readable face may still be tilted or propped on another tile: confirm the target rests flat with its broad face exposed. If a vertical press stalls against a tilted tile, withdraw and change contact direction or grasp/reseat it; continued downward pressing can wedge it between neighbors. Track any disturbed non-matching tile for restoration.

Finish this stage before touching either side stack. With the pushing hand clear, recheck all three matching tiles together, including any tile that was previously judged flat. Use an oblique wrist view to distinguish a broad face resting parallel to the table from a face still sloping toward a neighbor. If one edge remains raised, move that tile into visible free space and settle it flat before drawing. Keep the replacement in its original stack during this check; late repairs to the exposed group after the draw do not establish that the required sequence was completed.

### Draw and transfer in high clear air

Once the three matches lie flat, locate the two small side stacks of face-down tiles. The replacement is the upper tile of the stack nearer your robot body, on your side of the table. Distinguish the two stacks in head and wrist views before grasping, using the robot body and table edges to resolve near versus far. Grasp that nearer upper tile and verify its retention during the lift. Track the same tile through the transfer and placement. Reveal its patterned face during reorientation and plan the receiving grasp so the tile can be stood with its bottom edge free.

Resolve the face's top and bottom while the replacement is held high and visible. Compare its markings with an upright tile of the same pattern in the row, and keep track of the identified top edge through the handoff. A vertical rectangular outline and a face directed toward the robot do not distinguish an upright pattern from an upside-down one. If the pattern is symmetric or partly hidden, preserve the observed edge identity through the rotation and obtain a clearer view before choosing the supporting edge. Do not infer pattern orientation from the wrist quaternion alone when the tile may have shifted in the grip.

For this task, use a direct hand-to-hand exchange in high clear air instead of the generic tabletop-supported handoff procedure. Do not place the replacement on the table as an intermediate step to switch hands. Choose a mutually reachable transfer space visibly above the standing tiles, with room for both arms to approach and withdraw. Check the entire forearm and elbow sweep as well as the tile, fingers and wrist housings; lifting the tile alone does not clear a low arm crossing the row. Choose height together with reachability rather than repeatedly raising a stalled arm.

Keep the donor steady while presenting an exposed gripping area that the receiver can reach without contacting the donor's fingers or forearm. Confirm opposing receiving-pad contact in head/wrist views before easing the donor open. Watch that the tile stays fixed in the receiving grip as support transfers; a close command alone does not confirm this. Withdraw the donor clear before the receiver lowers or turns toward placement. If approach is blocked, retreat the receiver while the donor retains the tile, then adjust the offering pose or arm separation in the high clear workspace before trying again. Repeated interference calls for a changed approach, not a tabletop transfer or the same overlapping motion.

### Stand the replacement as a row extension

Before lowering, compare the held tile with the standing neighbor at the row's free right end using head and wrist views. Confirm the identified pattern top points upward and the correct bottom edge will contact the table. Correct an upside-down pattern while securely held in clear air before the final descent. Align its bottom edge with the row's tabletop baseline, its patterned face with the row's facing direction, and its spacing with the neighboring tiles. Resolve depth and rotation from another view if the head image makes a separated or skewed tile look aligned. Keep the whole gripper and forearm clear of the row throughout reorientation and descent.

Lower upright until the bottom edge visibly rests on the table, release gradually, and withdraw along a clear path without dragging the tile or its neighbor. Inspect again with fingers clear: the tile should remain upright, its pattern right way up, and continue the row's alignment, facing and spacing. If release leaves a visible offset, gap, lean or inverted pattern, regrasp and reseat from the observed discrepancy instead of repeating unchanged completion holds.

Check all three exposed matches, the preserved non-matching tiles, the supported row-end replacement and empty hands before native completion verification. After unexpected row movement, re-observe affected tiles and plan their restoration while preserving any held tile's support. Reserve time for replacement placement and any needed restoration. If completion remains absent after settling, apply the common observation-grounding rule to a specific unresolved relationship before another check.
