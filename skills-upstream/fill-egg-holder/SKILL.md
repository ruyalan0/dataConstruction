## Track actual seating

Survey current RGB for the basket, four eggs, four cup openings, hinge and lid free edge. Maintain a four-cup ledger: empty, released-but-unverified, seated, or perched. A stationary egg overlapping a cup in head RGB proves neither depth nor seating. Confirm from an oblique wrist view that the lower egg body enters the cavity, the rim surrounds it below its broad part, and the egg is stable after the empty fingers withdraw. An elongated egg lying across opposite rim edges remains perched even when its center projects inside the opening. Count only verified seating. Preserve seated neighbors during repair.

Prefer back cups before front cups for access. Inspect basket-wall clearance while selecting pickup order; a rim-constrained egg needs an approach that reaches its broad sides, and should not be left to a long series of late-budget retries. Plan four placements, possible seating repair, lid closure and home within the supplied 700-step budget.

## Localize the egg and cup separately

Use current images and the supplied geometric tools. Historical basket/table egg-center heights around 0.803/0.784 m and cup-plane height around 0.77 m are initial calibration hypotheses, not measurements after an egg rolls or sits on a rim. A repeated projection at guessed heights adds no evidence. When height or offset matters, match one identifiable physical surface feature across current views for triangulate_point; inspect its quality and infer the center offset separately. A silhouette center in two views is not necessarily the same surface point. If correspondence is ambiguous, obtain one clearer wrist view before committing.

The kinematic finger contact-center estimate is not the carried egg center. Re-estimate the egg-to-pad offset after wrist rotation, asymmetric finger motion or reseating. Cup alignment must follow the observed egg body and its lower end, rather than the nominal hand center alone.

## Grasp without trapping a finger against the basket

At clearance, orient the closing axis across the egg's short axis. Use an aperture just wider than the visible egg, allowing both pads to descend beside its widest section without squeezing against the basket wall. Inspect the achieved depth before closing. An open-command finger compressed inward, a wrist descent residual, or a pad stopped above the broad body indicates interference; closing harder at that same height tends to pinch the tip and eject or drop the egg.

Close while stationary, then perform a short vertical lift and a small lateral retention test over the basket. Require egg-to-pad stability while the background moves; nonzero gripper width alone can be basket contact. When the egg slides past the fingertips and the measured opening collapses, keep the basket as the catch area, open, withdraw and relocalize.

After two shallow misses/slips at the same support, change the access constraint before another pickup: choose the opposite arm or a side that clears the rim and admits both pads beside the equator. A yaw/roll change counts only if it visibly changes that clearance. If the rim still prevents a grasp, one short observed inward roll/push toward a visible empty basket interior is a candidate, followed by relocalization and a fresh grasp. Use a defined catch area and stop on the first displacement; its benefit is unverified. Avoid cycling through nominally different rolls while the same finger remains blocked.

## Lower the egg into the cup, then verify

Keep the held egg and wrist attachment clear of the rim and lid during transit. With the egg at clearance, inspect its long axis and opening width. An egg carried sideways can bridge a cup: rotate incrementally toward a more upright presentation of its lower end, preserving grip and rechecking retention and center offset. Select the arm and approach side that leave room for the attachment as well as the egg.

For back cups, approach from the open front with an observed tilt if the upright lid blocks the attachment. A lower front waypoint followed by a short inward advance previously improved access, but fixed pitch angles are not a seating prescription. If the lid moves or fingers become strongly asymmetric, retreat to clear space and correct geometry. Release only when the actual egg lower end is over the cavity with a visible inward settling path; lid contact is not evidence that release alignment is adequate.

Lower in short segments until RGB shows the lower end entering the opening and the cup taking support. Historical hand-center release heights around 0.875/0.845 m produced mixed outcomes and must not terminate descent by themselves. Open without simultaneous arm motion, let the egg settle, then withdraw clear and inspect from an oblique view. Update the ledger only after this inspection. In particular, unchanged egg position relative to a cup during withdrawal verifies release, not sufficient seating depth.

Repair a perched egg before collecting another egg or closing the lid. Identify the support edge and the empty cavity below. Prefer an accessible short regrasp at the broad sides, a small lift clear of the rim, and reorientation/recentering followed by an observed supported descent. A small contact toward the visible cup interior is an alternative only when it can clear the identified support without disturbing a neighbor. Observe after each contact; stop as soon as the egg moves, re-localize, and verify depth again. Downward force on an egg spanning two rim edges can wedge it instead of seating it.

## Close and finish

After four confirmed seated eggs, locate the hinge and free edge. Close through short observed contacts along the hinge arc, withdrawing to inspect when the view is obscured. If the lid rests on an egg, reopen enough to inspect and repair that egg's seating; extra lid force is not a placement correction. Confirm the lid lies closed and clear of fingers, return both empty open hands to their original home, then request native completion verification.

Use the episode's demonstrated clear-space approach/retreat geometry to avoid recalibrating each transit. Keep contact checks short and reserve time for the lid and home. Compare recent actual actions after a failed attempt and choose a physically distinct correction, rather than consuming the remaining budget with the same shallow grasp.
