name: pour-by-language
description: Use the empty arm's wrist view during bottle pickup, inward pouring, draining and upright recovery; route by destination side and grasp the broad middle body.
---

Resolve every source bottle and destination bowl from the live instruction and visible identities. Before moving, record each pair's source side, destination side, pickup arm, pouring arm, need for handoff and status (not started, staged, pouring, draining or empty-confirmed). Liquid appearing in a bowl is evidence of delivery, not evidence that the source bottle is empty.

1. **Choose the pouring arm from the destination half.** Ground left, center and right in the robot's workspace using the two arm bases and current head view/public geometry, rather than a moving wrist camera's image left/right. Classify both the bottle's current support position and the bowl opening. Prefer the left arm for a bowl in the left half and the right arm for a bowl in the right half: that arm must have room to bring the mouth over the bowl, raise the base to near inversion and recover upright. For a central bowl, choose the arm with the clearer complete tilt-and-return sweep. A central bottle can be picked directly by the selected pouring arm when the upright approach is clear. Recheck sides if an object moves. The easiest arm for the initial pickup is not automatically the pouring arm.

2. **Use the empty wrist to observe the pickup, then grasp the broad middle body.** Before each bottle pickup or staged regrasp, identify nearby bowls, especially those already containing liquid. Keep the pickup hand at a clear pre-approach while positioning the other EMPTY arm at an elevated oblique side view. Its wrist camera should show the bottle body, approaching fingers/wrist and the closest bowl rim together. Inspect the observer's own swept space first; its hand and forearm must stay outside bowls, the pickup corridor and later bottle/stream paths. Choose one useful nearby view rather than repeatedly circling the scene. Once established, hold the observer still while the pickup hand approaches in short segments, inspecting that wrist view together with the head view after each near-object segment. Verify actual clearance before lowering beside a filled bowl or closing. A bowl shifting, rocking, or tilting is a stop cue: unload along the last clear approach and revise the route; good wrist tracking does not cancel visible contact. If the clearance is occluded, establish a view or approach that resolves it before advancing. After the bottle is retained and lifted clear, withdraw the observer through clear space for transport; establish its pouring view at the clear pre-pour position in step 4. At a supported handoff, the donor can become the observer only after release and confirmed separation.

   Identify the bottle base, main broad body, shoulder, narrow neck and mouth. Place opposing finger pads around the middle of the broad body, approximately halfway between its base and shoulder, clearly below the neck. Open enough to clear that body diameter before approaching. Keep the fingers and wrist away from the mouth so they neither obstruct flow nor crowd the receiving rim during tilt. Do not default to the easier-to-reach narrow neck or just below the mouth. Verify body-level pad placement in current RGB before closing, then perform a short upright lift and compare bottle-to-pad position against the background. If the grip misses or the bottle pivots, return it to visible support and correct the middle-body grasp before transporting.

3. **Hand off opposite-side bottles before pouring.** A right-side bottle for a left-side bowl is picked by the right arm and passed to the left; mirror this for a left-side bottle and right-side bowl. Before pickup, identify a free flat tabletop patch in the arms' shared reach, with room for the upright bottle base, receiving fingers and both wrists. Inspect a clear overlap region rather than assuming the table center is empty; keep bowls and their liquid paths clear. The pickup arm carries the bottle upright to this support, lowers its base onto the table, opens and withdraws. Confirm the bottle stays upright and table-fixed with the donor fingers clear. Only then let the destination-side arm re-localize it, regrasp its broad middle body and pass a short retention lift. Preserve the bottle's identity and target bowl across the handoff. Transfer ownership in memory only after the receiving lift; an intermediate release is not a completed pour. Keep the donor in clear standby outside the receiving arm's sweep. If a held bottle's cross-body approach repeatedly stalls, return to visible support and perform this handoff instead of repeating the distant tilt. Budget the staging, regrasp, pouring, bottle return and final arm reset before committing to the route.

4. **Approach from the arm's side of the bowl and tilt inward.** Define left/right in the robot's workspace, independently of wrist-camera image axes. With the **left arm**, stage the bottle body on the **left side of the target bowl** and rotate the bottle's neck/mouth **rightward toward the bowl interior**. With the **right arm**, stage the body on the **right side of the target bowl** and rotate the neck/mouth **leftward toward its interior**. Apply this mirror rule to whichever bowl that arm is pouring into, including a central bowl and a bottle received by handoff. Plan the middle-body grasp orientation and wrist rotation for this inward sweep before committing to the side approach.

   **Establish the other arm's pouring view before the first tilt.** Pause the retained bottle upright at a clear outside pre-pour position. Bring the EMPTY arm to an oblique side view that exposes the bottle mouth, the receiving rim and landing area, and the gap between bottle/gripper and bowl. Use the head view as well to check the raised base and both forearms. Select the observing side from the actual geometry; it must stay outside the complete inward-tilt and upright-return sweep, nearby bottles, filled bowls and the falling liquid path. Keep the observing fingers open and clear. A pickup view is not automatically a useful pouring view: reposition once for this phase and check the actual wrist image before starting the preparatory tilt.

   Keep the bottle base clear of the table, other vessels and the receiving rim. An upright tall bottle cannot put its mouth near a low bowl without its body colliding: use the outside approach and a small preparatory inward tilt to raise the base, then bring the mouth over the bowl interior before liquid starts flowing. Make a short observed rotation to verify that the neck moves toward the bowl and the base rises into free space. Choose the wrist roll/pitch and coordinated arm motion from that actual response, rather than copying a signed joint angle or interpreting image-clockwise as inward. If the mouth moves away from the receiver, unload the tilt and correct the side/grasp orientation before resuming. Repeated wrist tracking stalls call for upright recovery and a better outside approach or supported regrasp, not larger commands at the same blocked joint.

   Aim for a low visible mouth-to-rim gap, roughly 1–3 cm when geometry permits, without touching the bowl. This is an estimated working clearance, not a fixed simulator threshold. Track the actual mouth throughout rotation, accounting for its offset from the middle-body grasp; the starting side is not a fixed mouth position as the bottle rotates. **Hold the observer stationary while the pouring arm moves in short increments**, and inspect its fresh wrist image together with the head view after each increment and flow hold. Watch for rim contact, bowl motion, a slipping bottle, or liquid landing outside the bowl or on an arm. On contact or bowl motion, stop advancing and reverse a short already-cleared segment to unload before revising the approach. If the view becomes blocked, pause further rotation with the mouth over the receiver and move only the observer through clear space to regain the view; if flow is missing the bowl, first reduce the pouring tilt to stop the spill. Resume only after checking the actual clearance and landing. A missing or occluded stream remains unknown, not evidence of an empty bottle.

5. **Observe flow and increase the inward tilt when it stops.** Continue the established inward rotation in short increments, compensating translation so the mouth remains above the receiving opening and the base can rise freely. Judge actual bottle inclination in RGB; wrist roll alone does not establish bottle pose after a slip. When liquid is visibly flowing into the intended bowl, briefly hold that pose and inspect again. If it misses or strikes the rim or an arm, reduce tilt to stop flow, recenter the mouth and clear the liquid path, then resume. Do not increase tilt to repair a missed landing.

   If the stream pauses or disappears before the bottle is almost inverted, keep the mouth over the same bowl and increase tilt another short increment. If flow resumes, hold and observe; when it pauses again, repeat. Do not return upright merely because the first stream ended, a few drops appeared or the receiving bowl now contains liquid. If the mouth is occluded, obtain a clear view: an unseen stream is unknown, not stopped.

6. **Confirm empty at near inverted vertical.** Continue the draining sequence past horizontal until the bottle's long axis is nearly vertical again with its **base above its mouth and its mouth pointing downward**. This is almost upside down, not the starting upright posture or a roughly horizontal bottle. Keep its mouth over the bowl and hold briefly. Use successive fresh observer-wrist images to inspect the mouth for residual flow, with the head view checking the bottle's inclination and clearance. If drops or a stream still emerge, allow them to drain and observe again. Only mark empty-confirmed after near inverted posture and no outgoing liquid persist across successive clear observations separated by a short stationary hold, with prior visible delivery into the correct bowl. A single no-flow image at an intermediate tilt is insufficient. If near inverted posture cannot be reached, the view remains blocked or no delivery was established, record incomplete/uncertain rather than asserting the bottle is empty.

After confirming empty, keep the observer in its clear side position while rotating the bottle back upright. Inspect the bottle base's downward sweep and its clearance from the rim, nearby vessels and both arms; keep the mouth over the receiver long enough to catch residual drops. Maintain the body grip. Once the bottle is upright and clear of the bowl, withdraw the observer through clear space before transporting the bottle back to stable table support. Release and verify it stays upright before withdrawing the holding hand. Complete the same full draining check for all language-selected pairs, preserve the filled bowls, then return both arms to their supplied original poses. Budget the observation setup, draining and arm return within the remaining native steps; do not mark an unfinished bottle complete to move to the next one.
