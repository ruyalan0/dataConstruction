---
name: sweep-blocks
description: Stage the brush for a supported handover into a usable sweeping grasp, then verify bristle contact with short test strokes and collect blocks with an interior margin.
---

Identify the handle, working bristle face, dustpan mouth, inclined lip, interior floor and side walls in current RGB. Follow the required left-to-right brush transfer using a deliberate supported handover. Before the left hand sets the brush down, plan the RIGHT hand's next grasp and low sweeping stroke. Choose a clear tabletop patch comfortably reachable by both hands, away from the blocks and pan mouth. Aim to set the brush with bristles down and its rigid back above them, the solid handle grip region exposed toward the right hand's approach, and room for open fingers beside the handle. Choose the handle direction so the receiving wrist and forearm can stay above the table while the bristles reach the surface; placing it at the far edge of reach or merely making the handle visible is insufficient.

While retaining the brush, orient it over the clear patch, then lower in short observed segments until supported. Use a stable resting face: if bristles-down placement rocks or tips, arrange a stable side rest with the exposed handle permitting a small, clear rotation into bristles-down after pickup. Avoid a bristles-up placement that would require another large inversion beside the pan. Open the donor on support, withdraw without dragging, and observe the settled brush. Re-localize its actual handle and face after release; the pre-release estimate may no longer apply. If settling hides the handle or leaves poor finger clearance, arrange a better supported pose before attempting the receiving grasp.

With the donor clear, approach the exposed solid handle with the right fingers already aligned for the working pose. Grip a substantial handle segment rather than the thin end around its hanging hole, keep the bristle head free, then make a short test lift and check retention. Inspect the actual brush: the bristles should be exposed below the rigid back, and the wrist should have clearance to lower them to the table for a short stroke. Wrist angles or apparent handle capture alone do not establish this. Make only a small clear-space correction if needed; if the received grip leaves bristles above the wrist, hides the working face or blocks descent, set it back on the clear patch in a revised receiving pose and re-grasp. Establish this usable right-hand brush grasp before the left hand picks up the dustpan and occupies the sweep corridor.

Hold the dustpan with the left hand, upright and supported by the table. Align its mouth with an accessible block and stabilize the handle. Keep the lip low enough to cross and leave a view along it into the interior. Start with one block, placing the bristles on its far side relative to the pan. Use a view that resolves both height and lateral overlap; an overhead overlap alone does not prove contact. Approach outside the block, then lower to the working height without pressing the rigid brush back or gripper onto the block.

Before a long sweep, make a short inward TEST stroke (about 5–10 mm when visually resolvable). Require the block to move toward the pan relative to the table/pan, not merely relative to the moving wrist camera. A low wrist tracking error, an executed command, or a block disappearing behind the brush is NOT proof of useful contact. If occluded, lift the brush clear without dragging backward and inspect the block again. If it stayed in place, reset outside it and correct the bristle-tip height OR cross-stroke alignment from fresh views; change one at a time and re-test. Do not repeat full strokes across the lip when the previous stroke moved only the brush. If two corrections still produce no block motion, inspect tool slip/working-face orientation and change the setup or supported grasp.

Once the test visibly moves the block, extend the same useful low stroke in short observed segments, preserving bristle contact as the block crosses the lip. Stop and re-establish contact if the block stops following. Continue into the interior while keeping clear of back and side walls. A block touching the mouth or resting on the outer ramp is not collected: verify its whole visible footprint lies well inside, preferably one block width from the mouth when space permits. Reposition and make another short inward stroke for an edge-straddling block; avoid pushing it out a side opening. Repeat for the remaining blocks, inspecting the group from a view resolving front/back depth. Withdraw without dragging blocks out, place the upright dustpan stably to the left of the brush, release the tools and return home as required.

Evidence: the 2026-09-23 r2 L20 trial spent most of its budget on transfer and brush orientation; late supported placement was followed by unsuccessful re-grasps. The operator requests staging the brush in a pose that facilitates the other arm's grasp and subsequent sweep. This candidate plans the supported transfer before pickup of the pan; it preserves short contact tests and interior margins. No successful execution is claimed; resolve poses and contact from current RGB and public feedback.
